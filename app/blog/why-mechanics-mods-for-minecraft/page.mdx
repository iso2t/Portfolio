# Why Mechanics Mods For Minecraft?

I absolutely HATE hard games. When I sit down to play, I'm usually trying to unwind.
Having to worry about every little thing while managing my actual life can start to
feel like work. I play for escapism, whatever form that may take.

So why do I keep making mods that make Minecraft harder?

I like what happens when a small addition changes how you approach something you
already do. That's what I was thinking about when I started making
[Heavy Inventories](/heavy-inventories) around 2016, when I was a junior in high school.
Picking up items is great and all, but what if you had to think about what you were
bringing home?

Picture a mining trip. You bring tools, food, and supplies, leaving some carrying
capacity for whatever you find. Then you come across another vein of iron. Do you
take all of it, or leave some behind so you can carry that redstone too? You have to
decide what matters to you at that moment. Keep collecting everything, and getting
home becomes part of the problem.

That interests me. One small mechanic can change how you prepare, what you collect,
and when you decide you've had enough.

## How would Mojang do it?

I keep coming back to this question: **if the Mojang developers added this feature,
how would they do it?**

How would it interact with the other mechanics? What would need to be balanced?
Could existing items or enchantments give players ways to deal with it? I want
players to understand the mechanic through things they already know about Minecraft.
Those are questions I've sat with for the last ten years while working on Heavy
Inventories.

Carrying weight isn't a new concept. Hell, Skyrim inspired me to make the mod in the
first place. I think the first release was around Minecraft 1.9, and at the time I
didn't know of another mod doing the same thing. That gave me room to figure out how
I wanted it to work in Minecraft.

The systems I make do add difficulty. But when I'm designing them, these are the
things I care about:

1. They should interact with Minecraft's existing items, equipment, and mechanics
   in ways players can understand.
2. Other developers should be able to build on them, with clear integration points
   for their own mods.
3. They should fit into the normal flow of playing: preparing, exploring, gathering,
   and coming home.

## Difficulty vs. Tedium

This is a line I've been thinking about for years. If a mechanic is so annoying that
I wouldn't want to use it myself, that's a good reason to reconsider it. I'm not
your father, parental figure, or bossy sibling. I don't want to give you chores while
you're trying to enjoy a game.

Heavy Inventories has a concrete example of that balance. Carrying too much can
make getting home difficult, but the [Surefooted enchantment](/heavy-inventories/enchantments#surefooted)
lets you keep walking with an overloaded inventory. You still have to manage your
load, and your equipment gives you another way to deal with it. That's the kind of
interaction I want to explore.

Configuration matters here too. People enjoy different amounts of friction, and
the same settings won't suit every player or modpack. Being able to adjust capacity
and penalties gives people some control over that experience.

I still want the defaults to feel good. A player should be able to install the mod
and get an understandable set of decisions out of it. Configuration gives them room
to tune those decisions to the way they want to play. I can get into the details of
that in another post.

What keeps me interested in making these mods is the experience those little
decisions create. I want a mining trip to give you something to think about before
you leave, while you're underground, and on the way home. Then you get back, put
away what you collected, and start thinking about the next trip.
